These data describe every entrance and exit to the NYC subway system,
with one row per entrance rather than per station. The variables I
retained identify the station (`line`, `station_name`, and the station’s
latitude and longitude), the routes it serves (`route1` through
`route11`), and characteristics of the entrance itself: whether it
allows `entry`, whether there is a `vending` machine, the
`entrance_type` (stair, elevator, escalator, door, and so on), and
whether the station is `ada` compliant. My cleaning steps were to read
in the CSV, convert the variable names to lower snake case with
`janitor::clean_names()`, select the variables listed above, coerce the
route columns to character so they share a type, and convert `entry`
from the character values “YES” and “NO” to a logical variable using
`case_match()`. The resulting dataset has 1868 rows and 19 columns.

These data are not tidy. The eleven route columns hold the same kind of
information spread across many columns, so a single variable (the route
served) is stored in eleven places rather than one. Most of these
columns are also empty for most entrances, since few stations serve more
than two or three routes.

clean_trash = function(df, wheel_label) {
  df |> 
    janitor::clean_names() |> 
    filter(!is.na(dumpster)) |> 
    mutate(
      across(any_of(c("dumpster", "year", "weight_tons", "volume_cubic_yards",
                      "plastic_bottles", "polystyrene", "cigarette_butts",
                      "glass_bottles", "plastic_bags", "wrappers",
                      "sports_balls", "homes_powered")),
             ~ suppressWarnings(as.numeric(.x))),
      trash_wheel = wheel_label)
}

trash_wheel_df = 
  bind_rows(mr_trash_df, professor_trash_df, gwynnda_trash_df) |> 
  select(-starts_with("x")) |>

These data record each dumpster of trash collected by three water wheels
in the Baltimore harbor. Each row is one dumpster, identified by the
`trash_wheel` that collected it, its `dumpster` number, and the `month`,
`year`, and `date` of collection. The remaining variables measure what
was collected: the total `weight_tons` and `volume_cubic_yards`, counts
of specific items such as `plastic_bottles`, `cigarette_butts`,
`wrappers`, and `sports_balls`, and the number of `homes_powered` by the
resulting energy. For each sheet I read only columns A through N to
exclude the note columns, dropped rows without a dumpster number,
coerced the count variables to numeric, and added a variable identifying
the wheel; for Mr. Trash Wheel I also rounded sports balls to the
nearest integer. Combining the three sheets gives 1269 observations
across 15 variables. Not every wheel tracks every item, so columns like
`sports_balls` are missing for the wheels that do not record them.
