These data describe every entrance and exit to the NYC subway system,
with one row per entrance rather than per station. The variables I
retained identify the station (`line`, `station_name`, and the station’s
latitude and longitude), the routes it serves (`route1` through
`route11`), and characteristics of the entrance itself: whether it
allows `entry`, whether there is a `vending` machine, the
`entrance_type` (stair, elevator, escalator, door, and so on), and
whether the station is `ada` compliant. My cleaning steps were to read
in the CSV, convert the variable names to lower snake case with
`janitor::clean_names()`, select the variables listed above, coerce the
route columns to character so they share a type, and convert `entry`
from the character values “YES” and “NO” to a logical variable using
`case_match()`. The resulting dataset has 1868 rows and 19 columns.

These data are not tidy. The eleven route columns hold the same kind of
information spread across many columns, so a single variable (the route
served) is stored in eleven places rather than one. Most of these
columns are also empty for most entrances, since few stations serve more
than two or three routes.
